Rogue Cards · Illustration-first layouts

Flexible art-to-text proportions, inspired by the uploaded black-and-white card reference. Large original engravings will dominate; different card types use different internal composition while retaining one battered ivory-and-ink visual identity. This is a design study only, not the finished deck.

Three-Room playtest architecture · Earlier small-illustration comparison

NEW DIRECTION: The picture should be the card's centre of gravity. No universal fixed art height or triple-row rules container. Illustration occupies 50–70% of most cards; concise effects live at the bottom, longer d6 behaviour is allowed more space. For example, a Room can have its rules in a narrow right-hand column over a large scene, whereas an Item needs only a short text band.

Illustration space is content-dependent

LayoutIllustration heightUse when…
Illustration dominant86 mm (66%)One short effect: Item, Companion, simple Feature
Large art72–81 mm (55–62%)Hero, Event, Room with compact effects, Trap
Monster62 mm (48%)Needs statistics and four d6 behaviours
Chest / dense56–59 mm (43–45%)Two alternative actions with risks and rewards
Split Room78 mm (60%)Map/room illustration with compact instructions alongside

Title may overlap the illustration on an opaque ivory paste-on strip. Rules stay on a separate clean paper area, with no text over dark art. No algorithmic micro-fonts: if a card becomes too verbose, edit its effect or use a Feature card, not a tiny font. All art is placeholder until original engravings are created.
