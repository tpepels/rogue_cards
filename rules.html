ROGUE CARDS

HOW TO PLAY
1 OR 2 HEROES · 2 CORE PAGES + DECK RECIPES

You are adventurers exploring a strange dungeon. Every Room might contain a Monster, treasure, a trap or something much stranger. You cannot predict what comes next, but you can decide which risks to take and which Items to save.

1. Your quest

Your goal is to descend through the dungeon, reach the Amulet Vault and escape with the Amulet. You win when you claim the Amulet and at least one Hero survives. If everyone is defeated, the expedition ends in failure.

In Everything Goes, resolve 12 UPPER, 12 MIDDLE and 12 DEEP non-Corridor Rooms before reaching the Vault. Corridors add extra visits without counting toward the twelve. Trapdoors can shorten the route.

2. What you need

  • One Hero card per player (play alone or with two Heroes).
  • The UPPER, MIDDLE and DEEP Room cards, and Monsters from each depth.
  • The shuffled Object deck (Items and Potions), plus the Vault and two Warden cards.
  • One six-sided die (d6) and twenty-sided dice (d20) for tests and attacks.
  • Additional d20s to show each Hero's and each Monster's remaining health.

The dice used to show health are counters. Never roll HP dice.

3. Meet your Hero

Each Hero has a starting Item and a special ability. Its four numbers tell you what it can do:

  • HP - Health Points: how much harm the Hero can withstand.
  • ATK - Attack: how well the Hero hits Monsters.
  • DEF - Defence: how well the Hero avoids being hit.
  • WIT - Wits: how well the Hero handles tricks, negotiations, magic and other challenges.

When you lose HP, lower your health die. Healing raises it again, but never above your starting HP. At 0 HP, remove the die: that Hero cannot act unless another Hero heals them before the expedition moves on.

4. Set up the dungeon

  1. Choose a recommended or custom deck (see pages 3–4), then choose one or two Heroes. Set aside each Hero’s printed starting Item.
  2. Set each Hero’s health die to its starting HP.
  3. Shuffle the remaining Objects, then draw one additional Object per Hero. “Draw an Item” means draw from this pile, including Potions. Keep discarded Items in one pile, newest on top.
  4. Shuffle the Rooms into separate UPPER, MIDDLE and DEEP piles. Shuffle the Monsters into three matching piles.
  5. Set the Amulet Vault and the two Warden cards aside. Begin with the UPPER Rooms.

5. Explore a Room

Play through one Room at a time, working together if there are two Heroes. In Everything Goes, resolve 12 non-Corridor Rooms per depth. A Corridor does not count, but its non-Corridor destination does. Finish required detours before descending; do not add extra visits after the twelfth non-Corridor Room.

  1. Reveal the Room. Read its introductory instruction first. If it tells you to draw a Monster or do something on entry, do so now.
  2. Make the Room's choice. Most Rooms offer two printed options. Choose one you can perform, pay any cost, and follow its instructions. If the option asks for a die roll, use the result printed on that card.
  3. Resolve any Monster. Fight it or attempt the alternative on its card, such as PARLEY (talking or bargaining). Finish the encounter before leaving.
  4. Leave the Room. Discard it unless it says KEEP. Reset abilities marked “once per Room.” Continue with the next Room.

PEEK means look at the next cards without moving them. REVEAL means show them face up to both players. A route choice tells you which Room to enter and where the others go; otherwise put revealed cards back in their original order. The Vault is never part of a route choice.

KEEP a Room face up beside the party for its later effect. Tucked Items cannot be spent until recovered. PACT is a special KEEP choice: the party may make only one Pact per expedition. Even after use, keep that Room sideways as a reminder; non-Pact choices remain available.

Example. You draw Flooded Chamber. Read the Room, choose how to cross, and roll its d6 if that choice tells you to. You don't need to consult a separate list of Room effects: the outcomes are printed on the card.

6. Making rolls

A d6 has six sides; a d20 has twenty. “2d6” means roll two six-sided dice and add their results. For a Room's d6, find the range on that Room and do what it says.

Some challenges call for a WIT test: roll a d20, add your Hero's WIT and compare the total with the number on the card. In an opposed test, both sides roll a d20 and add the stated ability; the higher total wins.

ADV means advantage: roll two d20s and use the higher result. DIS means disadvantage: roll two d20s and use the lower. Together they cancel. Bonuses such as +2 WIT add 2 to a WIT roll, not permanently to the Hero stat. A DEF bonus raises AC. If a card grants ADV on a DEF roll, the attacker gets DIS instead; DEF-roll DIS gives the attacker ADV.

On a WIT test, a natural 1 fails and a natural 20 succeeds, even before bonuses.

Example: A WIT 4 Hero rolls 12: 12 + 4 = 16, beating WIT 15.

7. When a Monster appears

Draw one Monster only when instructed, even with two Heroes. Mark its HP. Fight one Monster at a time; put additional drawn Monsters face up in a waiting line. You may try its printed PARLEY before fighting. A peaceful departure normally gives no combat reward.

1. Hero turn. Each living Hero takes one action per round, in either order: attack, use an Item or ability, try Elbereth, or pass. Printed triggered abilities cost no extra action unless stated.

2. Monster response. After each Hero turn, if the Monster survives, roll its behaviour d6 and follow that result. It might attack, cause another effect, or do nothing. An attack targets the Hero who just acted unless stated otherwise.

3. Continue. The next Hero acts, then a new round begins. A defeated Monster cannot respond. Finish any waiting fights before leaving the Room.

8. Attacking and defending

Defence is a fixed target: Armour Class (AC) = 12 + DEF. The defender does not roll. Higher DEF makes you harder to hit; it does not reduce damage.

To attack: roll d20 + ATK. If the total equals or exceeds the target's AC, you hit. Roll d6 damage and subtract it from the target's HP. A natural 1 on the attack die always misses; a natural 20 always hits and adds an extra d6 damage. Monsters attack the same way.

Combat example · Two Heroes vs Will-o'-Wisp
Wisp · 9 HP · AC 16 · ATK +4
Warrior · ATK +6 with Sword · AC 15
Knight · ATK +4 · AC 17 with Shield
1 · Warrior attacks

Roll 13 + 6 = 19: hits AC 16. Damage: 2. Blood Rush costs 1 HP and adds 2 damage. Wisp: 9 → 5 HP.

Wisp responds · Flash (d6 = 6)

Attack with ADV: d20s 7 and 12; keep 12. 12 + 4 = 16 hits Warrior AC 15. Deal 3 damage. Next Hero attack has DIS.

2 · Knight attacks

DIS: d20s 18 and 10; keep 10. 10 + 4 = 14 misses Wisp AC 16.

Wisp responds · Spark (d6 = 3)

15 + 4 = 19 hits Knight AC 17. Damage: 4. Intercept costs 1 HP and prevents 3 damage. Knight loses 2 HP total.

3 · Next round: Warrior attacks

12 + 6 = 18 hits. Damage: 5. Wisp: 5 → 0 HP. It cannot respond.

After defeating a regional Monster, first resolve up to one printed REMAINS choice, such as SEARCH or EAT. Then resolve that Monster’s printed DEFEAT effect and any earned Room reward. Parley or escape does not trigger DEFEAT. Discard the Monster unless keeping its printed trophy; a trophy cannot also be searched or eaten.

9. Items, danger and surprises

Each Hero may equip one Weapon, Armour, Ring and Companion and carry other Objects. Outside combat, Heroes may exchange and equip them, but healing always requires a card effect. Choose an acting Hero for each Room choice; that Hero pays costs, takes damage and receives healing unless stated otherwise. A living Hero may use healing on a downed ally before leaving.

Scrolls are discarded after use. A used Wand is turned sideways until recharged. To OVERCHARGE, discard an upright Wand and lose d6 HP to obtain the result described on it. Potions reveal their effect on first use; identical Potions share that discovery for the expedition.

WITH means carried; EQUIPPED means in its slot. To discard, put an Item on its discard pile, newest on top. Discarding a kept Room ends its effect unless it is a spent PACT. You cannot choose a cost you cannot pay; voluntary HP costs cannot reduce you to 0. Only a result labelled TRAP counts as a TRAP.

For a permitted REROLL, pay the printed cost (1, then 2, then 3 HP for escalating rerolls). Use the newest result unless the card says to choose. Use/resolve 5–6 means apply that printed outcome without rolling. Results changed by ±1 stay within the die’s faces; a reversed d6 uses 1↔6, 2↔5, 3↔4.

A TRAPDOOR can skip remaining Rooms or descend early. Resolve its landing immediately; skipped Rooms give no rewards. Next applies to the next matching roll or encounter; here/this Room effects end on leaving. Keep a card face up or turned sideways when it tracks an ongoing effect.

If you become POISONED, your Hero's d20 rolls have disadvantage until the poison is removed or the Room ends.

10. ELBERETH: a last resort

Instead of attacking, a Hero may try to engrave Elbereth to stop a Monster. Roll d20 + WIT against 15. On success, cancel the Monster's next response, or discard an Item to escape it without a reward.

On failure, that Monster immediately responds with ADV where applicable (instead of its normal response for this turn). A second attempt against it has DIS; failing summons another Monster, subject to the active-Monster limit. Sentinel and Warden ignore Elbereth.

11. The Amulet Vault

After DEEP, enter the Vault and roll its OMEN d6. Normally you must either FIGHT the Warden or attempt to SEAL it with magic.

To SEAL, discard 3 MAGIC Items (or 6 with two Heroes) and make the WIT test printed on the Vault. Success claims the Amulet. Failure forces a fight, and the Warden's first attack has advantage.

In a fight, the Warden begins at 20 HP. At 10 HP or fewer, replace Phase I with Phase II, keeping remaining HP. Excess damage carries through the change; reducing it directly to 0 still wins. Rare earlier choices may unlock other endings after Phase I.

You win when at least one Hero survives and claims the Amulet. Escaping empty-handed is survival, not victory. If no Hero can continue, you lose.

THE RULES EXPLAIN THE SHARED GAME. THE CARDS PROVIDE THE SPECIFIC CHOICES, COSTS AND CONSEQUENCES. WHEN A CARD SAYS OTHERWISE, FOLLOW THAT CARD.
